§ 1 — Purpose

This document describes the structure and scope of DockBound's privacy and data protection compliance program. It is the entry point for any internal, investor, or regulatory review of how DockBound handles personal data.

It is not a legal policy document and it is not user-facing. It is a program-level reference that explains what we are doing, why, and how the pieces fit together. The Art. 30 records, DPIA, vendor agreements, policies, and tooling built under the checklist are the evidence that this charter has been executed.

This charter should be reviewed and updated whenever a new product surface is launched, a new data category is collected, or a material change in processing activity occurs.

Geographies in scope

DockBound is a US company (PandA Ventures, LLC) operating a nationally- and internationally-sold product. The following privacy regimes apply:

  • GDPR — applies to EU/UK data subjects who use the product (even if DockBound has no EU establishment; extra-territorial reach under Art. 3(2))
  • US state privacy laws — CCPA/CPRA (California), TDPSA (Texas), CPA (Colorado), CTDPA (Connecticut), and a growing multi-state baseline of 24 states as of August 2026
  • UK GDPR — post-Brexit equivalent; same obligations as EU GDPR for UK data subjects

Data out of scope

Port and ship data (port guides, DOCK scores, congestion schedules, weather, travel advisories) is not personal data under any applicable law — it describes places and conditions, not individuals. The entire port guide pipeline, DOCK scoring system, and port schedule index are out of scope for privacy compliance obligations. This scoping decision should be documented in the Art. 30 records.

§ 3 — Controller vs. Processor Determination Confirm with counsel

Working determination — accepted; counsel confirmation recommended before launch. The dual-role determination for roster data is accepted as DockBound's operating position. Counsel should confirm before advisor/agency terms of service are finalized, as DPA language will need to be embedded in those contracts.
SurfaceDeterminationRationale
DockBound consumer app Controller DockBound determines the purposes and means of processing traveler data (account, search history, booking context). No third party instructs how this data is processed.
Compass / Chartroom — advisor and agency contact data Controller DockBound collects and processes advisor/agency contact info for its own purposes (billing, product delivery, communications).
Compass / Chartroom — client roster entries uploaded by advisors (pre-login) Processor When an advisor uploads or manually enters a client's name, email, and travel details before that client has a DockBound account, DockBound is storing that data on behalf of the advisor (the controller). DockBound is not collecting it for its own purposes at that point. Requires a DPA-equivalent clause in advisor/agency terms of service.
Traveler self-registration (same person, post-login) Controller Once a traveler creates their own DockBound account, they are providing their name, email, and phone directly to DockBound for DockBound's own service purposes. At that point DockBound becomes controller for that record — independent of the roster entry the advisor created. The same individual may exist in both capacities simultaneously.
Travefy integration Not a data processing relationship The Travefy integration exchanges no personal data. The connection is established via the advisor's API key (a service credential, not personal data). The only identifier used is the advisor's own email as a lookup key — data DockBound already holds as controller. All content pushed to Travefy via the API is port guide and DOCK score data (non-personal, location-level information). DockBound is acting as a feature/tool within the advisor's own account, not processing any Travefy data subject's personal data.
Implication of the dual-role determination: Advisor and agency terms of service must include a data processing clause covering the pre-login roster entries (where DockBound acts as processor). This is the primary contractual gap to close. DSR tooling must also distinguish between the two record types — a traveler exercising their erasure right must trigger deletion of both the roster entry (held as processor, on the advisor's behalf) and the self-registered account (held as controller).

§ 5 — Compliance Obligations & Our Approach

Each area below maps to one or more sections of the implementation checklist. Status reflects checklist completion.

Data Mapping (Art. 30)
We will maintain a living record of all processing activities — what data, for what purpose, with whom, and how long. Owned by the program DRI; reviewed quarterly and on each new data collection change.
Not started — §1
Lawful Basis & Consent
Each data category has a documented lawful basis. Marketing and SMS require explicit opt-in consent. Cookie consent will be opt-in for EU visitors. Consent is granular, timestamped, and trivially withdrawable.
Not started — §2
Data Subject Rights (DSR)
We will build tooling to handle access, erasure, portability, rectification, and restriction requests within GDPR's 30-day SLA. An internal admin tool will allow support staff to execute requests without manual Firestore access.
Not started — §3 (largest engineering lift)
Vendor / Sub-processor DPAs
We will confirm signed DPAs with Google Cloud, Stripe, Travefy, Apify, and Twilio. A master sub-processor list will be maintained and available on request.
Not started — §4
International Data Transfers
Firestore data currently resides in the US. For EU data subjects, Google Cloud's SCCs provide the transfer mechanism. Same confirmation required for Stripe, Travefy, and Twilio. EU data residency will be re-evaluated if EU volume becomes significant.
Not started — §5
Security (Art. 32)
Firestore provides encryption at rest and in transit by default. We will conduct an access-control audit, confirm least-privilege for all sensitive secrets, and implement structured logging for unauthorized access attempts. Access reviews run quarterly.
Not started — §6
Breach Notification
A written incident response plan will cover detection, severity assessment, and 72-hour supervisory authority notification. A named DRI will be accountable. Direct user notification will be defined for high-risk breaches.
Not started — §7
DPIA
A DPIA is likely required given systematic profiling potential in advisor dashboards and B2B processing of client data on behalf of agencies. Will be completed before any EU go-live. The port/ship pipeline is confirmed out of scope.
Not started — §8
Retention & Deletion
Retention periods will be defined per data category and enforced via scheduled Cloud Functions. Manual cleanup is not acceptable — automated enforcement is required for defensible compliance.
Not started — §9
Documentation & Governance
A public privacy policy (separate consumer vs. B2B notices) and an internal data protection policy for staff will be produced. This charter is the program-level governance document.
Not started — §10
US State Privacy Laws
The DSR tooling built for GDPR covers ~90% of US state law requirements. The delta includes: opt-out of "sale"/sharing (CCPA), GPC signal recognition, "notice at collection" disclosures, and a consent architecture that supports both opt-out (ordinary data) and opt-in (sensitive data) models.
Not started — §11

§ 8 — Open Questions (2 remaining — for counsel)

  1. DPO requirement — assumed not required at current scale (no large-scale systematic monitoring; not a public authority). This assumption must be documented with counsel before launch and revisited annually. Assumed no — confirm with counsel
  2. Primary supervisory authority — DockBound likely has both UK and EU data subjects. Building to EU GDPR standard satisfies both regimes substantively (UK GDPR is nearly identical). However, for breach notification, the supervisory authorities are separate: the ICO for UK data subjects, and a lead EU DPA for EU data subjects. The lead EU DPA is determined by where DockBound has its EU establishment (or EU representative, if no establishment). Counsel must confirm the correct authority and whether an EU representative needs to be formally designated. Pending counsel
